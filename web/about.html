cc2juno

Turns the knobs on an ordinary MIDI controller into real-time edits for a Roland Alpha Juno 1, Alpha Juno 2 or MKS-50.

Those synths have no knobs to speak of, and they do not respond to ordinary MIDI CC messages either. Everything on the front panel is reached by paging through a menu, and the only way in from outside is system exclusive. Roland sold the PG-300 programmer to solve that. This does the same job with whatever CC controller you already own: it listens for the CCs your knobs send, converts each one into the sysex message for the tone parameter you assigned it, and passes anything it does not recognise straight through.

All 36 tone parameters are available. A controller with fewer knobs than that can give one knob up to choosing a layer, which swaps what every other knob does — so eight knobs and a layer selector can still reach the whole synth.

Configurations are ordinary text files. The one this page exports is the same file used by the command-line version and by the build for the Tulip Creative Computer, so a mapping worked out here will run on a Raspberry Pi with no screen attached, and one worked out there opens here.

Getting sound out of it

Two things must be set on the synth itself, and nothing works until they are: sysex receive must be ON (under the MIDI button on an Alpha Juno), and the synth's basic channel must match the synth channel set on this page.
